WHY BAR INSURANCE IS DIFFERENT

Running a bar in Ohio is one of the highest-liability businesses you can own.

Bars and nightclubs serve alcohol to the public. That single fact creates a liability exposure that most other businesses never face. Under Ohio's dram shop laws, a bar can be held liable for damages caused by a patron who was over-served — including car accidents, injuries to third parties, and property damage that happens long after the patron left your establishment.

Add in the physical environment — late nights, crowded spaces, the occasional altercation — and you have a business that requires specialty coverage that goes well beyond a standard commercial package policy.

Myers Insurance Group understands the bar and nightclub business. We work with carriers that write real bar coverage — liquor liability, assault and battery, and all the coverages that actually protect Ohio bar owners when something goes wrong.

The coverage gaps that close Ohio bars

No liquor liability. Ohio's dram shop laws make you responsible for what happens after a patron leaves your bar. Without liquor liability, one DUI accident can generate a claim that exceeds everything you own.

No assault and battery coverage. Standard GL policies exclude assault claims. A fight in your bar — even one you didn't start and couldn't control — generates an A&B claim your GL policy won't pay.

Insufficient property coverage. Bars have significant equipment investment — draft systems, POS systems, sound systems, TVs, furniture. Many owners are dramatically underinsured on property.

No business interruption coverage. A kitchen fire or water line break can close a bar for weeks. Without BI coverage, those weeks of lost revenue come entirely out of your pocket.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Bar insurance in Ohio — what you need to know.

Does Ohio hold bars responsible for what happens after a patron leaves?

Yes. Ohio's dram shop laws allow injured parties to sue a bar that over-served a patron who then caused harm — even if the harm happened hours later and miles away. This is why liquor liability is the single most important coverage for any Ohio bar. Without it, you're personally liable for everything a drunk patron does after walking out your door.

My current GL policy says it covers my bar — is that enough?

Almost certainly not. Standard GL policies exclude liquor liability and assault and battery — the two biggest exposures for any bar. If your policy doesn't have a liquor liability endorsement and assault and battery coverage, you have massive gaps that your current agent may not have disclosed.

What does Ohio require bars to carry for insurance?

Ohio liquor control regulations require minimum liability coverage for establishments holding liquor licenses. The specific minimums depend on your license type. Beyond the legal minimums, the practical reality is that minimum limits are rarely sufficient to cover a serious alcohol-related incident.

I have a bouncer — do they need to be on my Workers Comp policy?

Yes — if they're an employee. Security staff face real physical risk and must be covered under Workers Compensation. If you use contracted security through a security company, verify that company carries their own Workers Comp. If a contracted bouncer is injured and their employer doesn't have coverage, you could be held responsible.
