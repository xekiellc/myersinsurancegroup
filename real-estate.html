Dwelling Coverage (DP3)

THE MOST COMMON MISTAKE

Your homeowner's policy
doesn't cover your rental.

This is the most common and most costly mistake Ohio real estate investors make.

Using a homeowner's policy on a rental property

Homeowner's policies are written for owner-occupied residences. The moment a tenant moves in, your homeowner's policy excludes coverage for that property. If your insurer discovers the property is tenant-occupied, they will deny any claim and potentially cancel your policy retroactively. You need a landlord policy — also called a dwelling fire policy or DP3 — for any property you rent out, even occasionally.

No loss of rents coverage

If a fire makes your rental uninhabitable, you lose the rent while repairs happen — which can take months. Loss of rents coverage replaces that income. Most landlords don't know to ask for it, and most policies don't include it by default.

LLC ownership and insurance alignment

If you own your rental properties in an LLC, your landlord policy should be written in the name of the LLC — not your personal name. Misalignment between your entity structure and your insurance can void the liability protection your LLC was designed to create.
