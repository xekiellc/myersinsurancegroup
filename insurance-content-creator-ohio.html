WHY CONTENT CREATOR INSURANCE IS DIFFERENT

Your homeowner's policy doesn't cover your business. Neither does your personal auto.

Most content creators assume their homeowner's or renter's insurance covers their equipment and their business. It doesn't. Homeowner's policies have strict sublimits for business equipment — typically $2,500 or less — and explicitly exclude business liability and income loss. If your camera gear is stolen from your car, your personal auto policy won't cover it either.

As your audience and income grow, so does your exposure. A defamation claim from someone featured in your content. A copyright infringement claim from a music label. A brand deal dispute. A hacked account that costs you weeks of income. These are real risks that real creators face — and there are real insurance products built to cover them.

Myers Insurance Group builds Ohio content creator policies that reflect how you actually work — whether that's from a home studio, on location, at events, or online.

What your current policies are missing

Homeowner's sublimits kill equipment claims. Most homeowner's and renter's policies cap business equipment coverage at $2,500. A single camera body can cost more than that. Your gear needs dedicated inland marine coverage.

Media liability is almost never included anywhere. Defamation, copyright infringement, and invasion of privacy claims arising from your content aren't covered by any standard personal or business policy. Media liability is a specialty coverage that must be specifically added.

Cyber liability is not included in GL. Account hacking and data breaches require dedicated cyber coverage. A hacked channel or stolen subscriber list is a real business loss — and a real insurable event.

Fitness creators face specific professional liability. If you give fitness or nutrition advice and a follower injures themselves following it, that's a professional liability claim that standard policies don't cover.

Media Liability

Professional Liability

Cyber Liability

Covers account hacking, data breaches, ransomware, and cyber extortion. A compromised channel or stolen subscriber database is a real business loss — and a real insurable event. Covers recovery costs and lost income.

Business Interruption

Covers lost income when a covered equipment loss — theft, fire, damage — prevents you from creating content. If your camera is stolen and you can't post for three weeks, BI coverage replaces that lost revenue.

General Liability

Covers injury to guests, crew, or visitors on your set or at your studio location. If you film with guests, conduct interviews, or have people on location, GL covers the physical liability that creates.

Fitness Creator Professional Liability

Specific coverage for fitness and wellness creators who provide advice, training programs, or nutrition guidance. If a follower injures themselves following your content, professional liability covers the claim.

Home Studio Coverage

Your homeowner's policy excludes business use of your home. A dedicated home studio endorsement or separate policy covers your studio build-out, equipment, and business liability for home-based creator operations.

HONEST ANSWERS

What content creator insurance doesn't cover.

We believe in straight talk. Here's what no insurance policy can protect you from as a creator.

Platform demonetization

If YouTube demonetizes your channel or TikTok changes its creator fund, no insurance policy covers that lost income. Platform risk is business risk — the only mitigation is diversifying your income streams across multiple platforms and revenue sources.

Account bans and suspensions

A platform banning or suspending your account — even wrongfully — is not an insurable event. Platform terms of service violations and account actions are outside the scope of any insurance product.

Algorithm changes

No policy covers reduced reach or lost income from algorithm changes. These are business risks inherent to platform-dependent income — not insurable events. What insurance does cover is the physical and legal risks that are real and quantifiable: stolen equipment, defamation claims, hacked accounts, and lost income from covered physical losses.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Content creator insurance in Ohio — what you need to know.

My camera gear is worth $15,000 — is it covered under my homeowner's policy?

Almost certainly not at full value. Most homeowner's and renter's policies cap business equipment coverage at $2,500 — and that sublimit applies regardless of your total policy limit. $15,000 in camera gear needs dedicated inland marine coverage. Equipment coverage for creators is affordable and covers your gear anywhere in the world.

Someone is claiming I defamed them in one of my videos — am I covered?

This is a media liability claim — and it's exactly what media liability coverage is designed for. Without it, you're paying for your own legal defense out of pocket, which can be extremely costly even when the claim has no merit. Media liability covers your defense costs and any settlement or judgment up to your policy limits.

I create adult content — can I get insurance?

Yes. Adult content creators are running legitimate businesses with the same real insurance needs as any other creator — equipment coverage, cyber liability, professional liability, and business interruption. Myers Insurance Group doesn't judge what you create. We find the carriers that write coverage for adult creators and build policies that actually work for your business.

Do I need insurance if I'm just starting out and not making much money yet?

Equipment coverage makes sense from day one if you're investing in gear. A $3,000 camera kit is worth protecting regardless of your income level. Media liability and professional liability become more important as your audience grows and brand deals start. We help you build coverage that matches where you are now — with room to grow as your business does.
