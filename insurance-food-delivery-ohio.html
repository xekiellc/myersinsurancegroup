THE COVERAGE GAP EXPLAINED

Here's exactly when you're covered — and when you're not.

Every delivery platform — DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub, Instacart — provides some insurance coverage. But that coverage has a gap that most drivers don't understand until they're in an accident.

The gap exists between the moment you log into the app and the moment you accept your first delivery. During this "waiting period" — when you're available but not actively on a delivery — the platform's coverage is minimal, and your personal auto insurance is excluded because you're using the vehicle for commercial purposes.

The solution is a rideshare or delivery endorsement added to your personal auto policy — or a separate commercial auto policy that covers your vehicle during all phases of delivery work. Without it, you have a window of time on every shift where you're essentially uninsured.

The three phases of delivery — and your coverage in each

Phase 1: App on, no active delivery. You're logged in and available but haven't accepted an order. Platform coverage is minimal — typically only liability at very low limits. Your personal auto policy is excluded. This is the gap.

Phase 2: Order accepted, en route to pickup. Platform liability coverage activates at higher limits. But collision and comprehensive coverage for your own vehicle may still be limited or absent depending on the platform and your policy.

Phase 3: Food in car, delivering to customer. Platform coverage is at its highest during active delivery. But again — collision and comprehensive for your vehicle depends on the platform's specific policy terms.

The solution. A rideshare/delivery endorsement on your personal auto policy fills Phase 1 completely and supplements platform coverage in Phases 2 and 3 for your vehicle's physical damage.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Delivery driver insurance in Ohio — what you need to know.

DoorDash says they provide insurance — why do I need more?

DoorDash provides liability coverage during active deliveries — but it has gaps. During Phase 1 (logged in, no active order), coverage is minimal. Collision and comprehensive coverage for your own vehicle during delivery depends on the platform's specific terms and may require you to carry your own collision coverage first. A rideshare endorsement on your personal policy fills Phase 1 completely and ensures your vehicle's physical damage is covered throughout your entire shift.

I had an accident while delivering — my personal auto insurer denied the claim. What now?

This is the scenario most delivery drivers don't believe will happen to them — until it does. When your personal insurer discovers the accident occurred during commercial delivery activity, they deny the claim under the business use exclusion. At this point, you're looking to the platform's coverage for what they'll pay, and your own pocket for the rest. If you didn't have a rideshare endorsement, there's no retroactive fix. This is why the endorsement needs to be in place before the accident.

I drive for multiple platforms — do I need separate coverage for each?

No. A rideshare and delivery endorsement on your personal auto policy covers you across all platforms — DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub, Instacart, and any other delivery or rideshare app. The coverage follows your vehicle and your activity, not the specific platform. Full-time multi-platform drivers may benefit from a commercial auto policy instead of an endorsement — we help you determine which option fits your situation and budget.

How much does a rideshare endorsement cost?

A rideshare and delivery endorsement typically adds $15 to $30 per month to your existing personal auto premium — one of the best value coverages available for what it protects against. The alternative — a denied claim after an accident during delivery — can cost tens of thousands of dollars in uninsured liability and vehicle repair costs. We review your current auto policy and add the endorsement where it makes the most sense for your situation.
