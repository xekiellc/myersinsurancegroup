THE TRUTH ABOUT AIRBNB COVERAGE

Airbnb's Host Protection is not insurance. Here's what you actually have.

Airbnb offers hosts AirCover — their built-in host protection program. It sounds comprehensive. It isn't. AirCover has significant exclusions, sublimits, and conditions that leave Ohio hosts exposed in exactly the situations that matter most.

Your homeowner's insurance is the bigger problem. Standard homeowner's policies exclude commercial activity — and renting your home on Airbnb is commercial activity. The moment a guest is injured, your homeowner's insurer can deny the claim and potentially cancel your policy for undisclosed commercial use.

Real short-term rental insurance is a specific product designed for hosts who rent their properties on Airbnb, VRBO, and similar platforms. It covers the gaps in platform coverage and replaces the homeowner's coverage that evaporates when you have guests.

What Airbnb's AirCover doesn't tell you

It's not an insurance policy. AirCover is Airbnb's own guarantee program — not a licensed insurance product. It doesn't have the same legal protections as a real insurance policy.

Your homeowner's policy is voided. Most homeowner's policies exclude short-term rental activity. Your insurer can deny claims and cancel your policy if they discover you're renting on Airbnb without disclosing it.

AirCover has significant sublimits. The coverage amounts for specific categories of damage — jewelry, art, cash, collectibles — are far lower than the full value of what most hosts have in their homes.

Guest injury is your biggest exposure. A guest who is injured at your property has a liability claim against you personally. AirCover's liability protection has conditions and exclusions that may leave you exposed in exactly this scenario.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Airbnb insurance in Ohio — what you need to know.

Does my homeowner's policy cover Airbnb guests?

Almost certainly not. Standard homeowner's policies exclude commercial activity — and renting your property on Airbnb is commercial activity under most policy definitions. If a guest is injured and you file a claim, your insurer can deny it and potentially cancel your policy for undisclosed commercial use. Some insurers offer short-term rental endorsements that can be added to a homeowner's policy — but many don't. We review your current homeowner's policy and identify exactly what's covered and what isn't.

Airbnb says AirCover protects me — isn't that enough?

AirCover provides some protection — but it's not an insurance policy, and it has significant limitations. It's Airbnb's own program, subject to their terms and conditions, and it doesn't carry the legal protections of a licensed insurance product. The liability coverage has conditions that may not respond to all guest injury scenarios. The property damage coverage has sublimits and exclusions. For hosts who depend on rental income and have significant property value at risk, AirCover alone is not sufficient protection.

A guest was injured at my Airbnb and is threatening to sue — what happens?

A guest injury at your short-term rental property is a premises liability claim against you personally. If you have proper host liability coverage, your insurer defends you and pays any settlement or judgment up to your policy limits. If you're relying on your homeowner's policy, it will likely deny the claim. If you're relying solely on AirCover, the conditions of their program determine whether and how much they respond. Host liability insurance is the only coverage that gives you reliable, unconditional protection for guest injury claims.

I only rent my property occasionally — do I still need separate insurance?

Yes. The frequency of rental activity doesn't change your liability exposure — it only affects the premium you pay. A guest can be injured on their first night at your property regardless of whether it's your first rental or your hundredth. Your homeowner's policy excludes the activity either way. Occasional hosts actually face more risk in some respects — they're less experienced managing guest expectations and property safety, and they're more likely to be surprised by a claim they thought their homeowner's policy would cover.
