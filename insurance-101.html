THE GLOSSARY

The words on your policy,
decoded.

Search a term or filter by topic. If a word on your dec page isn't here, email us at hello@myersinsurancegroup.com and we'll explain it.

The Basics

Premium

The price you pay for your insurance policy. You can usually pay it all at once or in installments.

The Basics

Deductible

The amount you pay out of pocket on a claim before your insurance pays the rest. A higher deductible usually means a lower premium.

Example: Your car takes $4,000 in damage and your deductible is $1,000. You pay $1,000. Insurance pays $3,000.

The Basics

Limit

The most your policy will pay for a covered loss. Anything above the limit comes out of your pocket, which is why limits matter more than almost anything else on your policy.

The Basics

Claim

Your formal request to the insurance company to pay for a covered loss.

The Basics

Carrier

The insurance company that actually issues your policy and pays your claims. Also called the insurer or underwriter.

The Basics

Peril

A specific cause of loss, like fire, wind, hail, theft, or vandalism. Policies either list the perils they cover or cover everything except the perils they exclude.

The Basics

Underwriting

The process a carrier uses to decide whether to insure you and what to charge. Underwriters look at your claims history, property, business type, driving record, and more.

The Basics

Independent Broker / Agent

An agent appointed with multiple insurance companies who can shop your coverage across all of them. Works for you, not for one carrier.

The Basics

Captive Agent

An agent who can only sell one insurance company's products. If that company's price or coverage isn't right for you, a captive agent has nowhere else to take you.

Your Policy

Declarations Page (Dec Page)

The summary page of your policy. It lists who's insured, what's covered, your limits, your deductibles, your premium, and your policy period. It's the first thing we look at in a coverage review.

Your Policy

Policy Period

The dates your policy is in force, from the effective date to the expiration date. A loss outside those dates isn't covered.

Your Policy

Endorsement

A change to your policy that adds, removes, or modifies coverage. Also called a rider, especially on life policies.

Your Policy

Exclusion

Something your policy specifically does not cover. Most coverage gaps hide in the exclusions.

Example: Standard homeowners policies exclude flood damage. You need a separate flood policy.

Your Policy

Named Insured

The person or business listed on the dec page as the policyholder. If your rentals are owned by an LLC, the LLC should be the named insured.

Your Policy

Additional Insured

A person or business added to your policy who gets protection under it. Landlords, general contractors, and event venues often require you to name them as additional insureds.

Your Policy

Certificate of Insurance (COI)

A one-page document that proves you have coverage. Clients, landlords, and venues ask for it before you start work or sign a lease.

Your Policy

Non-Renewal

When a carrier decides not to offer you a new policy when your current one expires. An independent broker can move you to another carrier. A captive agent usually can't.

Home & Property

Replacement Cost

Pays what it actually costs to repair or replace damaged property with new property of like kind and quality, with no deduction for depreciation.

Home & Property

Actual Cash Value (ACV)

Pays replacement cost minus depreciation. On an older roof or older belongings, ACV can leave you thousands short of what it costs to replace them.

Example: A roof that costs $20,000 to replace might only be worth $8,000 on an ACV basis.

Home & Property

Dwelling Coverage

The part of a home or landlord policy that covers the structure itself: walls, roof, floors, and built-in systems.

Home & Property

Personal Property Coverage

Covers your belongings: furniture, clothes, electronics, and other contents. Renters insurance is mostly this, plus liability.

Home & Property

Loss of Use

Pays extra living expenses, like a hotel and meals, if a covered loss makes your home unlivable while it's repaired.

Home & Property

Scheduled Personal Property

High-value items, like jewelry, art, or collectibles, listed individually on your policy with their own coverage amount. Standard policies cap these items at low limits unless they're scheduled.

Home & Property

DP3 (Dwelling Fire Policy)

The standard policy form for rental properties. If you rent a property out, a homeowners policy won't cover it. You need a landlord policy like a DP3.

Home & Property

Loss of Rents

Replaces the rental income you lose when a covered loss makes your rental property unlivable.

Home & Property

Vacancy Clause

A policy provision that limits or suspends coverage if a property sits empty past a set period. Vacant properties need dedicated vacant property coverage.

Auto

Split Limits (25/50/25)

The three numbers on your auto liability: bodily injury per person, bodily injury per accident, and property damage per accident, in thousands of dollars. 25/50/25 is Ohio's legal minimum, and it's dangerously low.

Auto

Uninsured / Underinsured Motorist (UM/UIM)

Covers your injuries when the driver who hit you has no insurance or not enough of it. One of the most important coverages on any auto policy.

Auto

Collision

Covers damage to your own vehicle from hitting another car or object, regardless of who's at fault.

Auto

Comprehensive

Covers damage to your vehicle from things other than a collision: theft, hail, fire, vandalism, falling trees, and hitting a deer.

Auto

Medical Payments (MedPay)

Pays medical bills for you and your passengers after an accident, regardless of fault.

Auto

Rideshare Coverage

An add-on that covers you while driving for Uber, Lyft, DoorDash, or similar services. Personal auto policies typically exclude this activity.

Liability

Liability Coverage

Pays for injuries or property damage you cause to someone else, plus your legal defense if they sue you.

Liability

Umbrella Policy

Extra liability coverage that sits on top of your home, auto, or business policies. It kicks in when a claim blows past your other limits.

Liability

Bodily Injury

Physical injury to another person. Bodily injury claims, with medical bills, lost wages, and pain and suffering, are where liability limits get tested.

Liability

Professional Liability (E&O)

Errors and omissions coverage. Protects you when a client claims your professional advice or services caused them financial harm. General liability doesn't cover this.

Liability

Liquor Liability

Covers claims when a patron you served alcohol injures someone or causes damage. Ohio's dram shop laws make this essential for any business that serves alcohol.

Life

Term Life

Life insurance that covers you for a set period, like 10, 20, or 30 years. Usually the most coverage for the lowest price.

Life

Whole Life

Permanent life insurance that lasts your whole life as long as premiums are paid, and builds cash value you can borrow against.

Life

Universal Life

Permanent life insurance with flexible premiums and a cash value component that grows based on interest rates or market indexes.

Life

Beneficiary

The person or people who receive the death benefit. Keep it current: marriage, divorce, and new kids should all trigger a beneficiary check.

Life

Death Benefit

The amount a life insurance policy pays to your beneficiaries when you die.

Life

Cash Value

The savings component inside permanent life insurance. It grows tax-deferred and can be borrowed against or withdrawn.

Business

General Liability (GL)

The foundation of business insurance. Covers third-party bodily injury, property damage, and advertising injury claims against your business.

Business

Business Owner's Policy (BOP)

A bundle of general liability and commercial property for small businesses, usually at a better price than buying them separately.

Business

Workers Compensation

Covers employee medical bills and lost wages from on-the-job injuries. Required for most Ohio employers, and in Ohio it's purchased through the state's Bureau of Workers' Compensation.

Business

Commercial Auto

Covers vehicles used for business. Personal auto policies generally exclude business use, so one work accident on a personal policy can mean a denied claim.

Business

Business Interruption

Replaces lost income and pays ongoing expenses when a covered loss, like a fire, forces you to close temporarily.

Business

Inland Marine

Despite the name, it has nothing to do with boats. It covers tools, equipment, and property that moves around, like contractor gear on a job site.

Business

Cyber Liability

Covers costs from data breaches and cyberattacks: customer notification, legal fees, ransomware, and recovery. Standard business policies exclude most cyber claims.

Business

Employment Practices Liability (EPLI)

Covers claims from employees alleging harassment, discrimination, or wrongful termination.

Business

Garagekeepers

Covers customer vehicles while they're in your care at a repair shop, body shop, or dealership.

Business

Class Code

The code a carrier uses to classify your type of business for pricing. The wrong class code can mean overpaying, or worse, a denied claim.

INDEPENDENT VS. CAPTIVE

Who you buy from
matters as much as what you buy.

There are two kinds of insurance agents. One works for an insurance company. The other works for you.

Captive agent
One company. One answer.

A captive agent is contracted to sell for a single insurance company. Whatever that company offers is all they can offer you, even when another carrier would cover you better or charge you less.

Independent broker
Many companies. Your best option.

An independent broker is appointed with multiple carriers. We compare coverage and price across all of them and put you with the one that fits. That's how Myers Insurance Group has worked since 2008.

01
More options, better fit
Every carrier prices risk differently. The company that's cheapest for your neighbor may be the most expensive for you. More carriers means a better shot at the right coverage at the right price.
02
We work for you
A captive agent's job is to sell their company's policy. Our job is to find the policy that fits you, wherever it is.
03
One broker, for the long run
Rates change. Carriers leave markets. Your life and business change. With an independent broker, you keep the same person in your corner while the carrier behind you changes as needed.
04
Nobody's too different
Strip clubs, weed shops, tattoo shops, adult creators, vacant rentals. Many standard companies won't touch them. Independent brokers can reach the specialty carriers that will.

To be fair

Plenty of captive agents know their stuff. The limitation isn't the person, it's the structure: they can only sell what one company makes. An independent broker isn't limited that way, and that's the whole point.
