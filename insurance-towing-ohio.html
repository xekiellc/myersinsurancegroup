WHY TOWING INSURANCE IS DIFFERENT

Towing creates liability at every stop.

Towing companies operate in one of the most liability-intensive environments in the transportation industry. Every call involves a distressed vehicle owner, a damaged or disabled vehicle, active traffic, and the physical risk of roadside work. The combination creates exposure on multiple fronts simultaneously.

On-hook coverage is the coverage most towing operators don't know they're missing until they need it. Standard commercial auto covers your tow truck — not the vehicle you're towing. If the car you're hauling is damaged in transit, on-hook coverage is what pays for it. Without it, you're personally liable for the full replacement cost of the customer's vehicle.

The coverage gaps that hurt Ohio towing companies

No on-hook coverage. Commercial auto covers your tow truck — not the vehicle being towed. A vehicle damaged during transport is an on-hook claim. Without this coverage, you pay for the customer's car out of pocket.

Roadside workers' comp gaps. Roadside towing work is one of the most dangerous jobs in Ohio. Drivers working alongside traffic face real injury risk — Workers Comp is non-negotiable and must cover roadside operations specifically.

Insufficient GL limits. Property damage at tow sites — vehicles struck, guard rails damaged, property on the scene — requires adequate GL limits. Low limits leave you personally exposed on larger incidents.

No storage yard coverage. Vehicles stored in your lot overnight need specific coverage. Standard policies may not adequately cover vehicles in your care at your storage facility.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Towing insurance in Ohio — what you need to know.

What is on-hook coverage and why is it essential?

On-hook coverage specifically covers vehicles that are attached to your tow truck and being transported. If a vehicle falls off the hook, is damaged during transport, or is involved in an accident while being towed, on-hook coverage pays for the damage to the customer's vehicle. Standard commercial auto covers your tow truck — not the vehicle on it. Without on-hook coverage, every tow you complete is an uninsured liability exposure for the full value of the customer's vehicle.

A vehicle stored in my lot was broken into overnight — am I liable?

Potentially yes. When a customer's vehicle is in your storage lot, you have a legal duty of care for that vehicle. Garagekeepers coverage is what pays for damage to or theft from customer vehicles in your care — including vehicles stored at your facility after a tow. Without it, you're personally responsible for the full cost of the customer's loss, which can quickly exceed the towing fee by a factor of many times.

One of my drivers was hit by a passing car while hooking up a vehicle — are we covered?

Yes — if you have Workers Compensation. Being struck by a passing vehicle is the leading cause of death for tow truck operators in the US. Workers Comp covers your driver's medical expenses, lost wages, and rehabilitation costs. Ohio requires Workers Comp for employees — and for towing operations, it's not just a legal requirement, it's a life-or-death financial protection for your drivers and their families.

Do I need special coverage for impound operations?

Yes. Impound operations create specific liability exposure — vehicles towed without the owner's immediate consent, storage fees disputes, and the higher likelihood of vehicles being in damaged or disputed condition when towed. Garagekeepers coverage is especially important for impound operators, and adequate GL limits are essential for managing the higher-conflict customer situations that impound towing creates.
