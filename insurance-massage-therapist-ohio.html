WHY MASSAGE THERAPY INSURANCE IS DIFFERENT

Hands-on services create unique liability exposure.

Massage therapy is a licensed professional service involving direct physical contact with clients in a private setting. That combination creates liability exposure that goes significantly beyond what standard business insurance covers. Professional liability from technique errors, sexual misconduct allegations, and home visit liability all require specific coverage that standard commercial policies exclude.

Sexual misconduct coverage is not the same as admitting wrongdoing — it's protection against allegations, which can arise regardless of actual conduct. In an industry involving private, hands-on contact, the exposure to false or exaggerated allegations is real. Legal defense costs for these claims are significant even when the therapist did nothing wrong.

Myers Insurance Group builds Ohio massage therapy policies that cover your professional services, protect you against allegations, and address the specific risks of hands-on practice — whether you work in a spa, a chiropractic office, or a client's home.

Coverage gaps most Ohio massage therapists don't know they have

No sexual misconduct coverage. Standard GL and professional liability policies exclude sexual misconduct allegations. In a profession involving private physical contact, this is your highest-stakes exposure — and it requires specific coverage.

Home visit liability gaps. If you perform massage at clients' homes, your professional liability needs to extend to off-premises services. Some policies limit coverage to a fixed location.

Product liability for retail products. If you sell massage oils, lotions, or other products, you carry product liability exposure. A client who has an adverse reaction to a product you sold can make a product liability claim.

No coverage for independent contractors. If you rent a room at a spa or chiropractic office, the facility's policy covers the facility — not your professional services. You need your own policy regardless of where you practice.

WHAT WE COVER

Massage therapist coverage in Ohio.

A complete Ohio massage therapy insurance program covers your professional services, your clients, and your practice location.

Professional Liability

Covers claims from your massage services — a technique that causes injury, aggravation of an existing condition, or failure to achieve the therapeutic results a client expected. Your most important coverage as a practitioner.

Sexual Misconduct Coverage

Covers allegations of sexual misconduct during massage services. Standard GL and professional liability policies exclude this — and in a profession involving private physical contact, this coverage is non-negotiable.

General Liability

Covers slip-and-fall, property damage, and non-professional client injury at your practice location. The premises liability foundation every massage practice needs.

Business Property

Covers your massage table, equipment, supplies, and practice build-out against theft, fire, and covered perils. Important for therapists with their own dedicated practice space.

Product Liability

Covers claims from massage oils, lotions, and other products you use or sell. A client who has an adverse skin reaction to a product applied during your service has a product liability claim.

Home Visit Coverage

Extends your professional liability and GL coverage to client home visits. Many standard policies limit coverage to a fixed location — this ensures you're covered wherever you practice.

Workers Compensation

Required in Ohio for employees. Repetitive motion injuries and physical strain are occupational hazards for massage therapists. Non-negotiable for any massage practice with employed therapists.

Business Interruption

Covers lost appointment revenue when a fire or covered loss forces you to close temporarily. A massage practice depends entirely on bookings — BI coverage protects your income during forced closure.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Massage therapist insurance in Ohio — what you need to know.

Does Ohio require massage therapists to carry insurance?

Ohio State Medical Board licensing requirements for massage therapists include insurance provisions. Beyond licensing, most spas, chiropractic offices, and wellness centers require proof of professional liability insurance before allowing a therapist to practice in their facility. And practically speaking, one uninsured professional liability or misconduct allegation can cost far more than years of premiums — regardless of whether the claim has any merit.

Why do I need sexual misconduct coverage if I haven't done anything wrong?

Because allegations — not just proven wrongdoing — generate legal costs. In a profession involving private, hands-on physical contact with clients in a closed room, the exposure to false or exaggerated allegations exists regardless of your conduct. Sexual misconduct coverage pays for your legal defense when an allegation is made — which can cost tens of thousands of dollars before the case is ever resolved. It's not about guilt. It's about protection against the cost of defending yourself.

I work at a spa — doesn't the spa's insurance cover me?

The spa's policy covers the spa as a business — not you as an individual practitioner. Your professional liability exposure follows you regardless of where you work. If a client sues you personally for a technique injury or misconduct allegation, the spa's policy protects the spa's interests — not yours. Independent contractors and employed therapists at spas, chiropractic offices, and wellness centers all need their own professional liability coverage.

A client claims my massage aggravated their existing back injury — am I covered?

Yes — with professional liability coverage. Aggravation of a pre-existing condition is one of the most common professional liability claims in massage therapy. A client who claims your technique worsened their condition has a professional negligence claim. Your professional liability policy covers your legal defense and any settlement or judgment up to your policy limits. Without it, you're paying for your own defense out of pocket — and these cases can be costly even when you did everything correctly.
