WHAT WE COVER

Retail store coverage in Ohio.

A complete retail insurance package protects your inventory, your customers, your employees, and your income. Here's what Ohio retail businesses actually need.

General Liability

Covers slip-and-fall, customer injury, and property damage claims. Retail stores are high-traffic customer environments — GL is the foundation of any retail policy.

Commercial Property

Covers your building or leasehold improvements, display fixtures, equipment, signage, and inventory against fire, theft, vandalism, and covered perils.

Inventory / Stock Coverage

Your inventory is your business. Make sure your policy covers your stock at its full value — many retail owners discover they're dramatically underinsured on inventory after a loss.

Business Interruption

Covers lost revenue and ongoing fixed costs when a fire, flood, or covered loss forces you to close temporarily. Retail businesses can't afford weeks of closure without income replacement.

Workers Compensation

Required in Ohio for employees. Covers employee injuries — stocking shelves, operating equipment, and customer interaction all carry real physical risk.

Crime & Theft Coverage

Covers shoplifting losses, employee theft, burglary, and robbery. Standard property policies have sublimits for theft — dedicated crime coverage ensures proper protection.

Product Liability

If a product you sell injures a customer, you can be named in the claim even if you didn't manufacture it. Product liability protects retailers from claims that originate with their suppliers.

Cyber Liability

If you process credit cards or store customer data, you have cyber exposure. A data breach or POS system hack can generate significant notification and recovery costs.

Business Owner Policy (BOP)

Bundles GL and property into one cost-effective policy. A BOP is the right starting point for most Ohio retail stores — with additional coverages added based on your specific operation.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Retail store insurance in Ohio — what you need to know.

Is my inventory covered under my commercial property policy?

It should be — but many retail owners discover their inventory is significantly underinsured after a loss. Your policy's business personal property limit needs to reflect the full replacement value of your inventory at peak stock levels. Seasonal businesses especially need to review this annually. We review your coverage limits as part of any policy assessment.

A customer slipped in my store and is threatening to sue. Am I covered?

A slip-and-fall claim is exactly what General Liability covers. Your GL policy pays for the customer's medical expenses, legal defense costs, and any settlement or judgment — up to your policy limits. This is why GL is the foundation of any retail insurance policy and why having adequate limits matters.

I sell products I didn't manufacture — can I still be sued if one injures someone?

Yes. As a retailer, you are part of the distribution chain for any product you sell. Even if the manufacturer is at fault, you can be named in a product liability lawsuit. Product liability coverage protects you in these situations — and is especially important for retailers selling health products, supplements, or any item with physical use.

What's the difference between a BOP and a commercial package policy?

A Business Owner Policy bundles General Liability and Commercial Property into a single, usually cost-effective package designed for small to mid-size businesses. A commercial package policy offers more flexibility and higher limits — better suited for larger retail operations or those with more complex needs. We help you determine which structure is right for your specific store.
